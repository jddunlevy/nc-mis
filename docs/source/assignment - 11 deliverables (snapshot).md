# MIS 430 Final Project Deliverables

*See slide deck for assignment details.*

Note that this is everything your team turns in. You will build a lot of it gradually across the
group phase of the project. Your weekly status reports track progress on each, and TAs can review
drafts in office hours before the final due date.

## 1. Elicitation Report

The report your team puts together based on information gained from the interviews you conduct
with real potential users. Details are outlined in the assignment document. Should include:

- Who you talked to
- Why you chose those individuals
- What you learned
- How what you learned shaped your work moving forward

## 2. Statement of Work (SOW)

The project's defining document. Clearly outlines:

- The problem (grounded in your fieldwork)
- Scope
- Prioritized requirements
- Deliverables
- Roles
- Risks
- Reporting plan

Must use the provided template. **Submitted earlier in the group work phase** and graded with
feedback that should be incorporated into the final deliverable version.

## 3. Process Models: current and future state

Swimlane/process diagrams (as practiced earlier in the semester) showing both the process
surrounding your product in its current state, and the process once your solution is implemented.
Make sure you clearly show what your product actually changes.

## 4. Data Model: future state

An ER-style data model (as practiced earlier in the semester) underlying your product. Must
include key entities, attributes, and relationships, with any consequential assumptions noted.

## 5. Product Backlog & First Sprint

**Product backlog:** prioritized user stories ("As a… I want… so that…"), each MoSCoW-ranked,
with Must-haves estimated (story points or t-shirt sizing) and a one-line reasoning each.

**First sprint:** laid out from the top of the backlog, with a short justification of what you
pulled in and what you deferred (scope, dependencies). **Must include at least one AI-feature user
story.**

**Acceptance criteria** are required for all first-sprint stories and for your product's core AI
feature, wherever it sits in the backlog. Observable-condition criteria preferred, and include
what the AI does when it can't or shouldn't answer.

## 6. Harm & Guardrail Analysis

Outlines how your product could cause harm, and the guardrails (design decisions) that address it.
Include harm categories ranked by severity for your product, with proportional safeguards (as
practiced earlier in the semester).

## 7. User Testing Plan & Results

Your plan for validating the design with real users, and what you learned from running it.

- **The plan** (built in class, refined after): goals, target users, 2–4 tasks (phrased as
  non-leading), what you'll measure/observe (including at least one trust-calibration
  observation), success criteria, and how you'll use results.
- **The results** (after testing): who you actually tested with, what happened on each task
  (success/struggle/failure), what surprised you, what you'd change, and an honest statement of
  what you *couldn't* test (AI behavior/accuracy on a mockup). Graded on honest findings and
  translation to action — not on whether the design "passed."

## 8. Change Management Plan

How the product's introduction would be managed: rollout, adoption, resistance, and transition.

- **Stakeholder Impact & Strategy Analysis:** 4 stakeholders (at least 1 who benefits, at least 1
  who resists), each analyzed for how the change affects them (work, gains, losses, likely
  response) and given a tailored strategy (their concerns, how to communicate, how to involve
  them, support needs). Grounded in real fieldwork stakeholders where possible. Must address the
  AI-trust dimension for at least the key resister.
- **One-Page Rollout Plan:** a visual one-pager — who to engage first, communication
  approach/timeline, top adoption risks, how success is measured. Must be visual and concise;
  plain paragraph-style docs don't earn full credit.

## 9. BMAD Decision Log

Your team's running record of where you changed, overrode, or rejected BMAD's output, and why.
Compiled from what you documented in each weekly status report — you should not be generating new
information for this deliverable.

## 10. Interactive Mockup

Your "final product": a clickable/navigable mockup of your product's key screens, demonstrating
your core feature. Building working AI is not required; the mockup represents the intended
experience.

## 11. Final Presentation

Your team presents your work to the class.
